Socratic method

Core: Questions lead to deeper understanding than answers.

Six question types:

  • Clarification — "what exactly do you mean?"
  • Assumption probes — "what are you assuming here?"
  • Evidence — "what tells you that's the bottleneck?"
  • Perspective — "how would the consumer of this API see it?"
  • Implication — "if this fails at 3am, what happens?"
  • Meta — "are we solving the right problem?"

Paul & Elder (2006)

Generation effect

Self-generated information is remembered significantly better than passively received information.

fMRI confirms: generation activates broader brain networks.

Even a wrong attempt at a solution produces better learning than reading the correct answer.

Slamecka & Graf (1978); Rosner et al. (2013)

Zone of Proximal Development

— Vygotsky, 1978

Learning happens between "can do alone" and "can do with guidance".

Too easy → no growth. Too hard → shutdown.

The scaffold must fade.

A scaffold that never fades becomes a crutch.

Cognitive load — the twist

Kalyuga's Expertise Reversal Effect

Scaffolding that helps novices actively harms experts.

The same teaching technique can speed or stall learning depending on who's using it.

High complexity + low expertise → heavy scaffold.
Low complexity + high expertise → step back.

Kalyuga & Sweller — Expertise Reversal Effect

Desirable difficulties

Robert Bjork, UCLA

Conditions that slow initial learning often produce stronger long-term retention.

Four difficulties:

  • Spacing (vs cramming)
  • Interleaving (vs blocking)
  • Retrieval practice (vs re-reading)
  • Varying conditions

Students prefer the easy path — which feels effective but produces the worst long-term results.

Protégé effect

Teaching, or preparing to teach, produces significantly better learning than studying alone.

Three mechanisms:

  • Preparation — forces organisation
  • Retrieval — forces recall
  • Feedback — questions reveal gaps

"Explain this middleware chain to me like I'm new to Fastify."

Chase, Chin, Oppezzo & Schwartz (2009) Stanford

Automation complacency

Humans relying on automation systematically reduce monitoring, lose situational awareness, and suffer skill atrophy.

Documented in aviation (Air France 447), medicine, and now software.

Devs stop reading docs. Debugging instincts erode. Architecture thinking weakens.

Parasuraman & Manzey (2010); Addy Osmani — Google Chrome

The design

From research to skill.

Sensei combines three enforcement layers:

  • ⛩️ Behavioral rules — Socratic defaults in SKILL.md
  • ⛩️ Structural handoffs — TODO(human) stop-and-wait
  • ⛩️ UserPromptSubmit hook — survives long sessions

5-level scaffolding

L5 Full ScaffoldDeveloper is lost. Shape given, logic blank.
L4 Guided DiscoveryName the pattern class. Walk through steps.
L3 Socratic (default)Respond with questions, not code.
L2 Minimal HintTwo words. "Think about coupling."
L1 ObserverWatch silently. Only speak on critical mistakes.

Default to L3. Escalate when genuinely stuck. Fade as competence grows.

The TODO(human) pattern

Adapted from Claude Learning Mode.

  • For 20+ lines involving design decisions, Sensei inserts a TODO(human)
  • Only one marker at a time
  • Structured handoff: Context · Your task · Guidance
  • Sensei stops and waits
  • Post-contribution: one insight connecting their code to broader patterns. No praise.

Session reports

What Sensei writes when you toggle off.

# Sensei Session Report — 2026-04-09

## What Went Well
- Independently debugged the N+1 after one hint
- Correctly identified the cache race without guidance

## Growth Areas
- Knex subquery syntax still needed scaffolding
- Could explain WHAT the resolver did, not WHY

## Knowledge Gaps Detected
- [ ] GraphQL: DataLoader pattern

## Sensei's Note
> You're building velocity without foundation. The Knex gap is
> mechanical — practice will close it. The "why not just what"
> gap is more important. Keep asking yourself "why this pattern?"

Spaced retrieval

Gaps persist across sessions in <storage>/gaps.md.

On the next session, Sensei weaves retrieval in naturally:

"Before we start — you had a gap in DataLoader last time. Without looking, what do you remember about how it batches?"

A gap only closes when the developer demonstrates competence unprompted. Getting it right with help doesn't count.

What Sensei is NOT

  • ❌ Not paternalistic — it never auto-disables
  • ❌ Not a gamified XP system — no streaks, no levels
  • ❌ Not an assessment tool — it observes real work
  • ❌ Not coupled to any note app — plain Markdown out
  • ❌ Not a CV skill — but it writes gaps a CV skill can read

Celebrate understanding, not counts.
